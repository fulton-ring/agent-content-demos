Northgate Community Food Bank AI use policy

Draft for board review · Effective when the board approves it · Owner: Executive director, until the board names an owner · Next review: in 12 months

Why we have this policy. We want AI to take routine writing off our plate. We must protect SNAP applicants, donors and volunteers, and keep a real person for anyone who needs help.

Who it covers. Staff, contractors and board members. Whether volunteers may use AI is still open for the board.

Tools we use

  • Gemini (in Google Workspace): Drafting and editing only; no protected information.
  • New tools, add-ons and AI features. Ken (IT) approves new tools, plugins, AI agents and AI features in existing software, after checking what data they see and whether the vendor trains on it.
  • Accounts. Use your work Google account and turn off vendor training on what you type.

What you can use AI for

  • Everyone, now. Staff may use Gemini in a work account for work with no information about a real person. Volunteer use waits for the board's decision.
  • Draft newsletters and social posts.
  • Write general volunteer thank-yous; add names yourself afterward.
  • Draft grant boilerplate about our work.

Needs approval first

  • Donor information in an AI tool — Marcus (CEO)
  • Reports to funders or Feeding America — Your manager
  • Recall notices or allergen information — Your manager
  • Anything using a corporate partner's logo or brand — Your manager

Off limits

  • Never put this into an AI tool unless the tool is approved for it above: SNAP applicants' Social Security numbers, income, household members and immigration documents (state human services agreement); donor names and giving records in Salesforce; volunteer names, phone numbers, emergency contacts and court-ordered case details; mobile pantry sign-up names and zip codes; passwords and logins.
  • No SNAP application information goes into any AI tool.
  • No AI answers SNAP eligibility questions. A person answers the SNAP line.
  • No AI images of hungry people. Our photos are real, with consent forms.

Check everything before it goes out

  • Whoever sends AI-assisted work owns it. Check facts, names, numbers and tone first.
  • Pounds and people served come from the inventory system, never an AI draft.
  • Check recall and allergen details against the original notice.

Decisions about people

  • AI never decides who gets help, benefits or a volunteer role. Trained people decide.

Being open about AI

  • We follow the AI clause in our corporate partner's agreement.
  • If a funder or partner asks about our AI use, we answer honestly.

If something goes wrong

  • Tell your manager and Ken the same day. Reporting a mistake is expected, never punished.

Who owns this policy

  • The executive director owns this policy until the board names someone; Ken approves tools.
  • The board's executive committee approves changes. We review it in 12 months.

Open questions for the board

  • Do we approve or stop staff's personal ChatGPT and Claude use, and will we pay for any AI accounts next year?
  • Should AI reminder texts in the volunteer scheduling software stay on, and do we tell people when AI helped write what they get?
  • May volunteers, such as group leaders writing shift notes, use AI?
