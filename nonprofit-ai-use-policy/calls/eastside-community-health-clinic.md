> **Invented.** This is a mock call transcript written for this demo. Eastside Community Health Clinic, the people on the call and everything they say are fictional. No real organization was interviewed.

**Call, 30 minutes.** On the call: Dr. Okafor (medical director), Beth (chief operating officer), Jordan (patient services lead). Fulton Ring asking.

**Q: Tell us briefly who you are.**

Beth: Nonprofit community health clinic, two locations. Primary care, behavioral health, and a dental clinic at one site. About 60 staff, 9,000 patients a year, most on Medicaid or uninsured. We have a sliding fee scale. We're a HIPAA covered entity, obviously.

**Q: What AI tools are people already using, for what, and on whose account?**

Beth: Our electronic health record vendor is pushing an AI scribe that listens to visits and drafts the note. We have a signed business associate agreement with the vendor, and a three-provider pilot started last month. That's the only approved AI.

Dr. Okafor: Clinicians use OpenEvidence and UpToDate's AI search for clinical questions. No patient information goes in, it's just "what's the first-line treatment for X." Some use ChatGPT for patient education handouts.

Jordan: Front desk and patient services, I know people use ChatGPT to write letters, like appointment reminders or the sliding fee letters. I hope without names. And we use Microsoft 365, with no Copilot.

**Q: What information do you hold about people that would hurt someone if it leaked?**

Beth: Everything. Protected health information: diagnoses, medications, behavioral health notes, substance use treatment records, which have extra federal protection beyond HIPAA, HIV status, reproductive health. Immigration status comes up. Income documents for the sliding fee. Insurance numbers. Then staff files, and donors, which is a small list in a spreadsheet; some donors are also patients, which makes it sensitive.

**Q: Where would a mistake from AI do real harm?**

Dr. Okafor: Clinical decisions, full stop. AI can help a clinician look something up, but a clinician decides and signs. The scribe drafts, the provider reviews every word before signing. I've seen it put a medication in the note that we discussed and decided against.

Beth: Billing and coding. If AI suggests codes and they're wrong, that's a compliance problem with Medicaid. Sliding fee eligibility: a person decides, from the documents.

Jordan: Anything that goes to a patient in a language other than English. A third of our patients prefer Spanish. We have certified medical interpreters for visits, but written materials sometimes get run through Google Translate.

**Q: Who should say yes to something new, and who is accountable if it goes wrong?**

Beth: Our privacy and security officer, Nadia, approves any tool that could touch PHI, and it needs a business associate agreement first, no exceptions. I approve tools otherwise. Clinical uses also need Dr. Okafor. The board approves the policy; the compliance committee reviews it first.

Dr. Okafor: Problems go to Nadia. If it might be a breach, HIPAA has its own clock and Nadia runs that.

**Q: What would your donors, clients and community expect you to tell them?**

Dr. Okafor: Patients should be told when the scribe is listening, and be able to say no. We're doing that in the pilot with a verbal consent script.

Jordan: Patient handouts written with AI should be reviewed by a clinician and we don't really need to label them, it's our handout. But a chatbot on our website answering patients? Patients should know it's not a person. We don't have one.

Beth: Funders, mostly HRSA and state, haven't asked. They will.

**Q: What worries staff, and what are they hoping AI takes off their plate?**

Dr. Okafor: Providers hope the scribe gives them their evenings back. Charting is why people leave primary care. The worry is accuracy and liability.

Jordan: My team worries about being replaced by a phone bot. And they're not sure what they're allowed to do, so some use it secretly and some won't touch it.

Beth: Leadership worries about a breach. One PHI leak into a consumer AI tool and we're reporting it to federal regulators.

**Q: Anything else?**

Beth: Whether we roll the scribe out to all providers is a decision after the pilot, in about three months. And whether the behavioral health team uses the scribe at all. Behavioral health notes are a different level of sensitive, and those providers are split.
