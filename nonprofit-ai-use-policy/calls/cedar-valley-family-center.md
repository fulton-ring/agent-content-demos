> **Invented.** This is a mock call transcript written for this demo. Cedar Valley Family Center, the people on the call and everything they say are fictional. No real organization was interviewed.

**Call, 30 minutes.** On the call: Dana (executive director), Luis (operations manager), Priya (adult education coordinator). Fulton Ring asking.

**Q: Tell us briefly who you are.**

Dana: We're a family services nonprofit in a mid-sized county. About 85 staff and maybe 150 regular volunteers. Three programs: family case management, which is the biggest; adult education, so GED prep and English classes; and a youth tutoring program in two middle schools. Budget is around nine million, mostly county and state contracts, then foundation grants, then individual donors.

**Q: What AI tools are people already using, for what, and on whose account?**

Luis: Officially, nothing. Unofficially, a lot. We're on Microsoft 365, and we turned on Copilot for the development team and me as a pilot in the spring, eight licenses. Development uses it for drafting appeals and thank-you letters and summarizing grant guidelines.

Priya: My teachers use ChatGPT, mostly free accounts, some paid on their own card. Lesson plans, reading passages at different levels, practice questions for the GED. A couple use it to translate handouts into Spanish.

Luis: And Canva, which has the AI image stuff now. Communications uses that. Our donor database is Bloomerang and I think it has some AI features we haven't turned on. Case management runs on a system the county requires, and as far as I know nobody's putting anything from it into AI. As far as I know.

Dana: That "as far as I know" is why we're on this call.

**Q: What information do you hold about people that would hurt someone if it leaked?**

Luis: Case files, first. Families' names, addresses, income, immigration status sometimes, children's names, domestic violence history, mental health and substance use notes. That's all in the county system and our contract with the county has a confidentiality section. Then donors in Bloomerang: names, giving history, notes from major gift conversations, some of which are personal. Then the adult ed students: names, test scores, some are undocumented. The tutoring program has kids' names, grades and the school sends us attendance under a data-sharing agreement with the district.

Priya: The data-sharing agreement says we can't share student records with any third party. I'd assume ChatGPT counts.

Luis: And staff files, obviously, HR.

**Q: Where would a mistake from AI do real harm?**

Dana: Anything that goes to the county. Our contract reports have numbers in them that we get audited on. A grant report with a made-up statistic would be a disaster. Anything with a family's name on it. And I don't want AI anywhere near deciding who gets emergency assistance funds. We have a small flexible fund, and caseworkers decide with their supervisor.

Priya: Placement. We place adult students into class levels. Someone floated using AI to score the intake essay. I'm not comfortable with that.

Luis: Public statements. If we put out a statement about something in the news, I want a person writing it.

**Q: Who should say yes to something new, and who is accountable if it goes wrong?**

Dana: Luis approves tools. He already does for software. For anything touching client data, Luis and our privacy officer, which is also our compliance director, Grace. The board will want to approve the policy. Our board chair has asked about AI twice.

Luis: If something goes wrong, tell me and Grace the same day. If it's client data, Grace decides if it's a breach under the county contract.

**Q: What would your donors, clients and community expect you to tell them?**

Dana: Donors, I don't think they care if AI helped draft a thank-you, as long as a person signed it and meant it. But I wouldn't want a photo that's AI-generated passed off as one of our families. That's a line.

Priya: Students should know if a reading passage was AI-generated. Actually I'm not sure that matters. Let's say we label materials.

Dana: One foundation asked in their application this year whether we use AI in grant writing. We said "for editing." We should be honest about that going forward.

**Q: What worries staff, and what are they hoping AI takes off their plate?**

Priya: Staff worry they'll get in trouble for using it, so they hide it. And some worry it's a step toward cutting positions. They hope it takes care of the paperwork. Case notes are the biggest time sink.

Dana: Leadership worries about a data leak, and about something wrong going out under our name. I'd love it to help with reports. On case notes, I'm not ready. Notes are in the county system and I don't know what the county allows.

Luis: Can I add one? Someone asked about installing a ChatGPT plugin in the browser that reads your email. I said no but we don't have anything written.

**Q: Anything else?**

Dana: Training. If we have a policy, people need an hour on it, not just an email. And whether we buy ChatGPT Team or extend Copilot to everyone is a budget question for the board. We haven't decided.
