> **Invented.** This is a mock call transcript written for this demo. Bright Futures After School, the people on the call and everything they say are fictional. No real organization was interviewed.

**Call, 30 minutes.** On the call: Renee (executive director), Sam (site director, one of four sites). Fulton Ring asking.

**Q: Tell us briefly who you are.**

Renee: After-school and summer programs for about 400 kids, kindergarten through eighth grade, at four sites, three of them inside public schools. Fourteen full-time staff, and about 45 part-time group leaders, a lot of them college students. Funded mostly by a state 21st Century grant, the school district and a few foundations.

**Q: What AI tools are people already using, for what, and on whose account?**

Sam: Group leaders use ChatGPT and whatever's on their phone for activity ideas, STEM projects, games. Some of the college kids use it for everything. I use it to write the weekly parent newsletter and translate it into Spanish and Vietnamese.

Renee: Our attendance and enrollment system is a youth program platform the district set up. Office staff use Google Workspace for Nonprofits, so Gemini is available. We bought nothing. Our grant writer is a contractor and I assume uses AI. I've never asked.

**Q: What information do you hold about people that would hurt someone if it leaked?**

Renee: Kids. Names, photos, grades and test scores the district shares with us, attendance, IEP and 504 information for kids who need accommodations, allergies and medications, custody orders, who's allowed to pick up. Behavior incident reports. Our data-sharing agreement with the district is strict: student records stay in the platform, and only staff who need them. We also have a lot of photos and video of kids from activities.

Sam: And the incident reports. If a kid gets hurt or there's a behavior thing, the group leader writes it up.

Renee: Donors are small. A spreadsheet, honestly, of about 300 people.

**Q: Where would a mistake from AI do real harm?**

Renee: Anything about a specific child. Incident reports, notes to parents about behavior. Those are legal documents in a way. Mandated reporting: if a staff member suspects abuse, they call it in. That's never going near AI.

Sam: Translation. If I translate a newsletter and it says pickup is at 5 instead of 6, a parent shows up late and a kid is waiting. I have a parent volunteer check the Spanish but nobody checks the Vietnamese.

Renee: Our grant reports to the state. Outcomes data. That has to be right.

**Q: Who should say yes to something new, and who is accountable if it goes wrong?**

Renee: Me, for now. We don't have IT staff. Our operations manager, Joy, handles the systems and she'd be the one to check settings. For anything with student data, the district has to agree too, under the agreement.

Sam: If something goes wrong, tell your site director, and site directors tell Renee. Same day.

**Q: What would your donors, clients and community expect you to tell them?**

Renee: Parents would want to know if AI is being used with their kids. Like if kids are using a chatbot in the program. Right now they aren't, officially.

Sam: Some of the middle schoolers use it for homework help in our homework hour, on their own phones or school Chromebooks. The schools have their own rules about that.

Renee: No AI images of our kids. And no AI-edited photos of real kids either. And the translated newsletter should say it was machine-translated, so parents know to ask if something's off.

**Q: What worries staff, and what are they hoping AI takes off their plate?**

Sam: Group leaders want it for activity plans. That's the main thing. Site directors want help with the paperwork: schedules, newsletters, the weekly reports.

Renee: My worry is a group leader takes a photo of a kid's homework with their name on it and asks ChatGPT for help, or pastes an incident report in to "clean it up." They're 20 years old, they do this with everything. And I'm worried about the state monitor asking what our AI policy is and us not having one.

**Q: Anything else?**

Renee: Homework hour. Do we allow kids to use AI tools during program time, and do we follow each school's rule or make our own? I don't know yet. That one probably needs the board and the principals.
