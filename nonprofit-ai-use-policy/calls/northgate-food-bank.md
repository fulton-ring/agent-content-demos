> **Invented.** This is a mock call transcript written for this demo. Northgate Community Food Bank, the people on the call and everything they say are fictional. No real organization was interviewed.

**Call, 30 minutes.** On the call: Marcus (CEO), Tasha (volunteer and community programs manager). Fulton Ring asking.

**Q: Tell us briefly who you are.**

Marcus: Regional food bank. We supply about 120 partner pantries across four counties, run two mobile pantries, and a SNAP outreach program where staff help people apply for benefits. Thirty-two staff, and volunteers are the backbone: roughly 6,000 people a year come through the warehouse.

**Q: What AI tools are people already using, for what, and on whose account?**

Tasha: We're a Google Workspace shop. Gemini is in there now and some people use it in Gmail and Docs. I use ChatGPT, paid, my own account, for volunteer newsletters and social posts. Our volunteer scheduling software sends AI-written reminder texts, that's a feature that came with an update.

Marcus: Finance uses nothing that I know of. Our grants person uses Claude for first drafts. Our inventory system is from the national network, no AI in it.

**Q: What information do you hold about people that would hurt someone if it leaked?**

Marcus: The SNAP outreach program is the big one. People give us Social Security numbers, income, household members, sometimes immigration documents, to fill out applications. That's covered by our agreement with the state human services department. Then donors. We have a lot of small donors and some big corporate partners, it's all in Salesforce. Volunteers: names, phone numbers, emergency contacts, and for court-ordered community service volunteers, their case details.

Tasha: Pantry clients mostly don't give us names. The partner pantries collect that, not us. Except mobile pantry sign-ups, which have names and zip codes.

**Q: Where would a mistake from AI do real harm?**

Marcus: SNAP. If someone gets wrong information about eligibility from us, they lose benefits or get an overpayment they have to pay back. Our outreach staff are trained and certified. I would never want a chatbot answering eligibility questions.

Tasha: Food safety. Recall notices, allergen information on donated product. If AI summarized a recall wrong and a pantry handed out recalled food, that's harm.

Marcus: Numbers in reports to Feeding America and our funders. Pounds distributed, people served. Those come from the inventory system, not from anyone's draft.

**Q: Who should say yes to something new, and who is accountable if it goes wrong?**

Marcus: Our IT is one person, Ken, plus an outside firm. Ken approves tools. I'd want to sign off on anything that touches SNAP or donor data. The board's executive committee should approve the policy; the full board doesn't need to.

Tasha: Reporting problems: tell your manager and Ken. Same day.

**Q: What would your donors, clients and community expect you to tell them?**

Marcus: Corporate partners are going to ask. One already has an AI clause in their partnership agreement about how we use their logo and brand. Our community wants to be talked to by a person. If someone calls the SNAP line, a person answers.

Tasha: Volunteers get AI-written reminder texts already and nobody's complained. I don't think we need to label those.

Marcus: Images. We don't use AI images of hungry people. Ever. Our photos are real, with consent forms.

**Q: What worries staff, and what are they hoping AI takes off their plate?**

Tasha: Staff hope it handles the newsletters, the social posts, the volunteer thank-yous, the grant boilerplate. Warehouse staff don't care much either way. The worry is mostly from the SNAP team: they're afraid leadership will want a chatbot to replace them.

Marcus: Which I don't. I'm worried about the opposite: somebody pasting an application into ChatGPT to "help" and we've leaked a Social Security number.

**Q: Anything else?**

Marcus: We don't have budget for paid AI accounts this year. People will use Gemini because it comes with Workspace. Whether we pay for anything is next year's budget.

Tasha: Can volunteers use AI? Like our volunteer group leaders writing up the shift notes. I'd say yes for that but I don't know.
