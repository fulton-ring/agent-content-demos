Bright Futures After School AI use policy

Draft for board review · Effective when the board approves it · Owner: Renee, executive director · Next review: In 12 months

Why we have this policy. We want AI to save staff time on planning and paperwork. We must protect kids' information and keep the trust of families, the district and our funders.

Who it covers. Staff, group leaders, volunteers, contractors including our grant writer, and board members.

Tools we use

  • None yet: No AI tool is approved yet. Whether to approve ChatGPT or Gemini is a board question.
  • New tools, add-ons and AI features. Renee approves any new tool, add-on, plugin, agent or AI feature in software we have. Joy checks settings, and the district must agree if student data is involved.
  • Accounts. Use a work account, not a personal one, and Joy checks that vendor training on what you type is turned off.

What you can use AI for

  • Once a tool is approved, these are fine without asking, with no child information:
  • Activity plans, STEM projects and games for your group.
  • Drafting schedules and weekly paperwork.
  • Drafting the weekly parent newsletter and translating it into Spanish and Vietnamese.

Needs approval first

  • Any new AI tool, add-on, plugin or feature — Renee, executive director
  • Any AI use with student data — Renee and the district
  • AI help on state grant reports — Renee, executive director

Off limits

  • Never put this into an AI tool unless the tool is approved for it above: Kids' names, photos and video; grades and test scores; attendance; IEP and 504 information; allergies and medications; custody and pickup details; incident reports; the donor list; passwords and logins.
  • Never use AI for mandated reporting. If you suspect abuse, call it in.
  • No AI-made images of our kids, and no AI-edited photos of real kids.
  • Never paste a child's information into AI. Our district agreement keeps student records in the platform.

Check everything before it goes out

  • Read all AI output before it goes out. Check pickup times, dates, names and numbers.
  • Check outcomes numbers in state grant reports against our own records.
  • You are responsible for anything you send, even if AI wrote it.

Decisions about people

  • AI never decides who gets a place, an accommodation, or a consequence for a child.
  • AI may help plan general activities. It does not judge or write about one child.

Being open about AI

  • Label translated newsletters as machine-translated, so parents know to ask if something looks wrong.
  • We tell parents before kids use AI chatbots in our program.

If something goes wrong

  • Tell your site director the same day. Site directors tell Renee the same day.
  • Reporting a mistake is expected. No one is punished for telling us.

Who owns this policy

  • Renee, executive director, owns this policy. Joy checks tool settings.
  • Renee shares it with staff and group leaders.
  • We review it every 12 months.

Open questions for the board

  • Do we approve ChatGPT or Gemini for staff, and may group leaders use personal accounts?
  • Do kids get to use AI in homework hour, and do we follow each school's rule or make our own? (Board and principals)
  • Who checks the Vietnamese translation? Today only a parent volunteer checks the Spanish.
  • Do we tell funders, like the state, when AI helped with reports? Does the board approve this policy and later changes?
