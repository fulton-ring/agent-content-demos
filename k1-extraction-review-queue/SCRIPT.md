# Two-minute walkthrough script

This is for a founder recording the voice-over. The screen steps match `record.py`, so you can talk over `recording/walkthrough.mp4`, or open `output/index.html` and click through yourself at 1440×900. Each section gives the time, what's on screen, and what to say. It runs about 280 words.

---

**0:00 to 0:15.** The review screen opens with the documents on the left.

> These are real K-1s. Enviva and Blackstone publish sample K-1 packages for their unitholders, so nobody had to send us anything. We also made scanned copies, plus some made-up 1120-S, 1041 and brokerage 1099s.

**0:15 to 0:40.** Open *Enviva Partners 2021* and click box 1, then box 19 C*.

> Each value we pull out is tied to the box it came from. Click box 1, a loss of 8,913, and it's highlighted on the K-1. Same for the coded boxes, like distributions under code C.

**0:40 to 0:55.** Click box 20 \*, then *Open the statement*.

> When the issuer writes "STMT" instead of an amount, we don't guess. It goes to review with a link straight to the statement page.

**0:55 to 1:15.** Open *Enviva Partners 2021, office scan* and click box 1, then 17 B.

> Here's the same K-1 printed and scanned. We line the page up with the blank IRS form for that year, so we know where every box is. Then we read each box on its own, twice. If the two reads disagree, or there's ink we can't account for, a person looks at it.

**1:15 to 1:40.** Click the *Review queue* tab, correct Blackstone box 6b to 535, then confirm Enviva box 1.

> This is the queue. Each item shows the box cropped from the page and why it's here. This scan says 535 and the software read 935. Its two reads disagreed, so it never went through on its own. Fix it, confirm the next one, and move on.

**1:40 to 2:00.** Tick *Check against answer key* and open *Blackstone 2019, office scan*.

> We checked everything against a hand-keyed answer key. Born-digital K-1s came out exact. On scans the software hadn't seen while we tuned it, 99% of the values it accepted were right, and the queue caught 66 of 68 misreads. That's the job: the software does the reading, and your staff only look at what's uncertain.
